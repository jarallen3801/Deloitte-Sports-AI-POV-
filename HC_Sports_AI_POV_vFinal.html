AI is Disrupting the Sports Industry

AI is Disrupting the Sports Industry

Today more than ever, sports organizations sit at the epicenter of entertainment, navigating a complex set of stakeholders across the industry. As such, they hold an enviable position. Through thoughtful maneuvering, these organizations can convene and deliver value throughout a rich ecosystem of fans and consumers.

While these organizations sit in this position, they are also facing:

Rising fan expectations for real-time, personalized content and digital experiences

Rapid expansion of AI/ML capabilities across sports technology

Pressure to improve operational efficiency using new capabilities amid flat cost structures and increasing scrutiny

Greater competition requiring faster innovation and differentiation

Talent constraints accelerating automation and new ways of working

As the industry and technological landscape apply greater pressure for sports organizations to evolve at speed, tasks and work done by roles across sports organizations will predominantly fall into 3 categories:

AI-Assisted
Tasks will remain primarily human-owned, with moderate support from AI and automated solutions.
AI-Augmented
Tasks are more evenly split between human workers and AI and automation capabilities, with high levels of collaboration and hand-offs.
AI-Powered
Tasks are owned primarily by AI and automation solutions, with humans managing outputs and monitoring performance to drive continuous improvement.

1. Quantify the Opportunity

How can we quantify the AI opportunity?

At the work and workforce level, the potential disruption cuts across all functions and roles of sports organizations.

Our Deloitte workforce analysis shows that nearly 100% of roles in sports organizations have the potential to be disrupted to some degree by AI, with 43% of roles having a potential disruption of over 40%.

Deloitte's proprietary tool, Workforce Analyzer, estimates the total disruption potential of AI on the workforce and where a human-in-the-loop is most valuable.

Methodology: Our findings are derived from a mock sports organization built to capture the common roles and capabilities of an organization in the industry. This analysis should be considered directional until validated against specific client data. Opportunities will vary based on organizational maturity, shape, workforce composition, and other factors.

100% of roles in sports organizations face some level of AI disruption.

Professional and Support employees face higher disruption levels ranging from 45% to 53%, meaning that the workforce impact is concentrated at the layers that keep the day-to-day operations running.

~75% of disrupted roles retain high human necessity, signaling that the workforce challenge is less about replacement and more about redesigning how people and AI work together.

Overall AI disruption potential for roles ranges from 17.80% to 60%.

2. Humans x Machines Partnership

How can we envision the Humans x Machines partnership?

At the work and workforce level, the future is not humans OR machines, nor is it humans AND machines; it is a deliberate Humans x Machines partnership to create a multiplier effect. AI will not replace work uniformly; instead, tasks and roles will shift across the AI-assisted, AI-augmented, and AI-powered spectrum, depending on the level of judgment, complexity, and context required.

This partnership - defined by the strategic choices outlined in the section above - articulates how sports organizations should approach AI in their workforce, clarifying where humans lead, where machines augment, and where AI can execute with human oversight.

The focus is not just on deploying AI, but on determining the optimal mix of human necessity and machine-enabled execution across workflows. By rethinking work in this way, sports organizations can increase speed, consistency, and scalability while preserving the human capabilities that drive decision-making, creativity, and trust.

Human necessity, and the skills that complement it, continues to be a key component of understanding how AI will impact the workforce of sports organizations. We know that AI can assist, augment, and power nearly every role, but with all functions showing a material need for human necessity (scores ranging from 0.51 to 0.63), the question becomes how to prioritize and sequence AI opportunities and deployment across departments.

Where you start depends on how your organization prioritizes value and use cases. You can take a broad approach, deploying AI across the entire organization to build momentum and gain incremental value, or you can go deep within a specific function, concentrating efforts to drive meaningful impact in a targeted area or workflow.

The important thing is that you start with intention.
